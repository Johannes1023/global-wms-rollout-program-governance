# 🌍 Global WMS Rollout & Program Governance

## 📌 Project Overview

This program focused on the global rollout of Manhattan Associates Warehouse Management for a large international consumer company.

The transformation covered **16 countries, more than 2,000 end users and a program investment exceeding €10 million**. My delivery focus included rollout activities in Latin America, particularly Brazil, as part of a broader global deployment.

I worked as IT Program Manager within an international program organization, coordinating global workstreams, local market teams, implementation partners and business stakeholders.

My role covered program governance, rollout planning, risk and issue management, stakeholder alignment, change management, training and business readiness.

---

## 🎯 Business Challenge

The supply chain organization had historically operated in a highly decentralized way.

Regional and local teams used different warehouse solutions, processes and working practices. This created a fragmented technology landscape, limited process standardization and made it difficult to establish consistent ways of working across countries.

The program introduced Manhattan Associates WMS as a common global platform and standardized core warehouse processes across **16 markets**.

This represented a significant change for local organizations. Some markets had established their own processes and systems over many years and were reluctant to replace them with a centrally defined solution.

The challenge was therefore not simply to deploy new software, but to move a decentralized organization toward a common operating model while managing local requirements, cultural differences and resistance to change.

Key topics included:

- Global process and platform standardization
- Multi-country rollout planning and execution
- Local market adoption and organizational resistance
- Program governance and decision-making
- Risk, issue and dependency management
- Change management and end-user training
- Business readiness across more than 2,000 users

---

## 🧩 My Role

I worked as IT Program Manager between the global program organization, regional business teams, local markets and external implementation partners.

My focus was on translating the global rollout strategy into executable market plans and keeping multiple workstreams aligned against common milestones.

A significant part of my role was managing the organizational side of the transformation. Introducing a central WMS required local teams not only to learn a new application, but also to change established warehouse processes and ways of working.

## 🏛️ Program Governance

With several countries and workstreams progressing in parallel, strong governance was necessary to maintain control over the program.

The governance model created transparency across:

- Scope and milestones
- Market readiness
- Risks and issues
- Cross-workstream dependencies
- Required decisions
- Escalations
- Delivery KPIs

A key part of my role was distinguishing between issues that could be resolved within a local rollout and topics requiring decisions at regional or global program level.

This helped prevent local exceptions and unresolved dependencies from gradually undermining the global template.

---

## 🌎 Multi-Country & Cultural Complexity

The rollout involved teams with different operating models, organizational cultures and expectations regarding decision-making.

This was particularly visible when centrally defined processes met established local warehouse practices.

Typical challenges included:

- Different levels of process maturity
- Strong preference for existing local solutions
- Different decision-making and communication styles
- Language and cultural differences
- Competing local and global priorities
- Dependency on local key users and subject matter experts

My role was to create enough structure to maintain the global direction while giving local teams a clear channel for raising valid operational requirements.

---

## 🔄 Change Management & User Adoption

Resistance to the new platform was one of the major delivery risks.

For many employees, the rollout meant replacing familiar systems and processes with a standardized global solution.

Change management therefore started before go-live and included:

- Stakeholder communication
- Local change preparation
- Key-user enablement
- End-user training
- Business readiness tracking
- Feedback and issue resolution

Training was particularly important because it served two purposes: preparing more than **2,000 end users** for the new platform and identifying remaining process or readiness gaps before deployment.

---

## ⚠️ Program Risk & Dependency Management

The size of the program created significant dependencies between technology, business processes, vendors and local market activities.

Typical risks included:

- Delayed market readiness
- Resistance to global process standards
- Open local requirements
- Incomplete training
- Vendor or resource constraints
- Dependencies between global and regional workstreams
- Delayed decisions affecting rollout milestones

I used structured risk, issue and dependency management to make critical topics visible early and ensure that they reached the appropriate decision level.

---

## 📊 Program Tracking & Executive Reporting

With multiple rollout waves running in parallel, reliable management information was essential.

I maintained transparency across areas such as:

- Market rollout status
- Milestone achievement
- Critical risks and issues
- Training and readiness progress
- Cross-market dependencies
- Resource constraints
- Required management decisions

This provided the program leadership with a consistent view across markets instead of relying on fragmented local reporting.
